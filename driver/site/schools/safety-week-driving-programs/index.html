Safety Week

Safety Week Programs

A safety week has a fixed window and a fixed student body, which makes it a throughput problem before it is anything else.

The Program

What it actually is

Most safety weeks fail quietly the same way: a good activity that only forty students actually experienced, while the rest saw a poster.

So the format changes. Short sessions, several simulators, staff managing the queue, and a single clear message rather than a curriculum. The aim is that most of the year group actually drives rather than a lucky subset.

How we plan it

  • Start from the number of students and the hours available
  • Work back to how many simulators that requires
  • Keep sessions short enough that the queue keeps moving
  • Pick one message and demonstrate it well

Please note: Academy Sim Racing does not currently have simulators installed in Florida school districts. The programs described here are services Academy offers and can deploy. Any reference to a district on this site describes an area we can serve, not an existing installation or partnership.

What Academy is, and is not. Academy Sim Racing supplies racing and driving simulators and runs staffed programs with them. We are not a licensed driving school and we do not employ state-certified driver education instructors. Nothing we provide counts toward Florida licensing requirements, the supervised driving hours a learner must complete, or any state-required driver education course. Districts and schools should deliver those through their own certified staff or an appropriately licensed provider. What we offer is supplementary practice and hazard exposure alongside them.

Format

How it runs

01

One day, high throughput

Several simulators running continuously through the school day.

02

Across the week

Fewer simulators over more days, which suits schools that cannot free a whole day of scheduling.

03

Paired with an assembly

A short whole-group session to frame it, then simulator time in rotation.

Practical

Planning it

Give us the real numbers

Year group size and the hours genuinely available, not the optimistic version. It is the only way to size this correctly.

Scheduling is the hard part

Getting students to the simulators in an orderly rotation is usually harder than running the simulators. Involve whoever builds the schedule early.

Space and power

A gym or cafeteria works best for high throughput, because there is room for both the equipment and the queue. We need mains power at the setup point.

Questions

What districts ask

How many students can take part in a day?
It depends on session length and simulator count. Give us your year group size and available hours and we will tell you what is realistic.
Can you run across several days instead?
Yes, and for many schools that is easier to schedule than freeing a whole day.
What space works best?
A gym or cafeteria, because you need room for the equipment and for the students waiting.
Do you handle the rotation?
We run the simulators and keep the queue moving. Getting students to the room in order is normally the school schedule, and it is worth planning early.
Do you have this running in schools now?
No. Academy does not currently have simulators installed in any school district. This is a program we offer and can deploy.
How far in advance should we book?
As early as you can. Safety weeks cluster in the calendar and districts often need lead time for procurement anyway.

Bring this to your school

Tell us the school or district, student numbers and your timeframe. We will send options and costs.
