Defensive Driving

Defensive Driving Programs

For students who already have a license, the useful work is not learning to drive. It is unlearning the habits that six months of unsupervised driving has already built.

The Program

What it actually is

New drivers develop bad habits quickly and privately. Following too closely, checking mirrors too rarely, and a general assumption that other road users will behave predictably.

A defensive driving program targets those specifically, by putting a student in situations where the habit fails and letting them feel the consequence safely.

What we work on

  • Following distance. Almost everyone runs shorter than they think.
  • Hazard recognition. Seeing the developing problem rather than the current one.
  • Emergency braking. Most drivers do not brake hard enough in a genuine emergency.
  • Recovery. What to do when the car is already doing something you did not ask for.
  • Distraction. Demonstrated rather than described.

Please note: Academy Sim Racing does not currently have simulators installed in Florida school districts. The programs described here are services Academy offers and can deploy. Any reference to a district on this site describes an area we can serve, not an existing installation or partnership.

What Academy is, and is not. Academy Sim Racing supplies racing and driving simulators and runs staffed programs with them. We are not a licensed driving school and we do not employ state-certified driver education instructors. Nothing we provide counts toward Florida licensing requirements, the supervised driving hours a learner must complete, or any state-required driver education course. Districts and schools should deliver those through their own certified staff or an appropriately licensed provider. What we offer is supplementary practice and hazard exposure alongside them.

Format

How it runs

01

Single session

A focused session on one or two behaviors, which is usually more effective than covering everything shallowly.

02

Program across a term

Repeat visits building on each other, for schools that want more than a one-off.

03

Targeted groups

For students identified by staff as needing it, or for a driving club or CTE cohort.

Practical

Planning it

Pick a small number of behaviors

A session aimed at following distance and hazard recognition will change more than one that tries to cover the whole syllabus.

These students think they can drive

That is the useful part. The program works because the simulator can demonstrate otherwise without anybody getting hurt or embarrassed in front of a class.

Confirm what your district expects

If this is meant to satisfy a specific requirement, check that with your district first. Simulator time does not substitute for state-required instruction.

Questions

What districts ask

Is this for students who already drive?
Primarily, yes. The value is in addressing habits that have already formed.
Does it satisfy any court or district requirement?
Not that we can claim. If it is meant to meet a specific requirement, confirm that with your district before planning around it.
How many behaviors can you cover in a session?
Two, properly, beats six in passing. Tell us what concerns you most about your students and we will focus there.
Can staff observe what a student did?
Yes. What happened in a session is visible and can be discussed afterwards, which is often where the learning actually happens.
Do you have this running in schools now?
No. Academy does not currently have simulators installed in any school district. This is a program we offer and can deploy.
Can this run alongside a driver education course?
Yes, and it complements it well, because the audiences are different stages of the same journey.

Bring this to your school

Tell us the school or district, student numbers and your timeframe. We will send options and costs.
