The District

What we know about Pasco County

Pasco County Schools is headquartered at 7227 Land O’ Lakes Blvd. The district’s driver education course is delivered online through Pasco eSchool, which lists Driver Education S among its current high school electives.

We could not confirm a district-run behind-the-wheel program from public sources. That is a meaningful difference from neighboring districts and it shapes what would actually be useful here.

Source: Pasco eSchool high school courses.

Please note: Academy Sim Racing does not currently have simulators installed in Florida school districts. The programs described here are services Academy offers and can deploy. Any reference to a district on this site describes an area we can serve, not an existing installation or partnership.

What Academy is, and is not. Academy Sim Racing supplies racing and driving simulators and runs staffed programs with them. We are not a licensed driving school and we do not employ state-certified driver education instructors. Nothing we provide counts toward Florida licensing requirements, the supervised driving hours a learner must complete, or any state-required driver education course. Districts should deliver those through their own certified staff or an appropriately licensed provider. What we offer is supplementary practice and hazard exposure alongside them.

The Fit

Where a simulator would actually help

An online driver education course has an obvious structural limitation: there is no practical component at all. A student can complete it having never touched a steering wheel in any instructional setting.

That makes Pasco a genuinely different conversation from Hillsborough. It is not about adding hazard work to an existing practical program. It is about whether there is any hands-on element available to students taking the course at all.

Where it would fit

  • As the practical counterpart to an online course. A student who has completed the theory has somewhere to apply it.
  • Without needing a fleet. A simulator program does not require vehicles, certified behind-the-wheel instructors or a range, which is the whole reason those programs are hard to start.
  • Campus by campus. A mobile program can serve individual high schools without a district-wide commitment.
  • As an evaluation. A single day tells the district whether the format is worth pursuing further.

To be clear about what this is not: a simulator does not make a student road-ready and does not satisfy any licensing or driver education requirement. If the district wants a behind-the-wheel program, it needs vehicles and certified instructors, and we would say so.

If you fund driver education through the Dori Slosberg Act. s.318.1215 lets a county add $5 to civil traffic penalties to support driver education, and requires that programs receiving those funds devote at least 30 percent of student instruction time to behind-the-wheel training. Simulator time is not behind-the-wheel time and cannot count toward that 30 percent. We would rather say so now than have it surface during an audit.

Questions

What districts ask

Does Pasco County offer driver education?
The district lists Driver Education S as an online course through Pasco eSchool. We could not confirm a district-run behind-the-wheel program from public sources.
Do you have simulators in Pasco schools?
No. Academy does not currently have simulators installed in any school district. This describes what we can provide.
Could a simulator replace behind-the-wheel training?
No. If the district wants a behind-the-wheel program it needs vehicles and certified instructors. A simulator adds a practical element to a course that currently has none, which is a different thing.
Would this need a vehicle fleet?
No, and that is much of the appeal here. A simulator program needs floor space and power rather than cars, instructors and a range.
Can you serve a single high school rather than the whole district?
Yes. A mobile program can run campus by campus without a district-wide commitment.
How would a school start?
A single day on one campus is the usual way. It costs little and answers the question.
