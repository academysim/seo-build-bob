The District

What we know about Pinellas County

Pinellas County Schools is headquartered at 301 Fourth St. SW in Largo. The district runs driver education as after-school camps, normally about one per quarter and subject to instructor availability, and documented a 2025 summer program across ten high school sites with a $250 lab fee.

District purchasing records show driver education vehicles leased at scale: approximately 27 vehicles for 13 high school sites, and approximately 57 for 2024 summer sessions across ten sites. That is a real, funded program with a real fleet.

Source: district driver education page and board purchasing documents.

Please note: Academy Sim Racing does not currently have simulators installed in Florida school districts. The programs described here are services Academy offers and can deploy. Any reference to a district on this site describes an area we can serve, not an existing installation or partnership.

What Academy is, and is not. Academy Sim Racing supplies racing and driving simulators and runs staffed programs with them. We are not a licensed driving school and we do not employ state-certified driver education instructors. Nothing we provide counts toward Florida licensing requirements, the supervised driving hours a learner must complete, or any state-required driver education course. Districts should deliver those through their own certified staff or an appropriately licensed provider. What we offer is supplementary practice and hazard exposure alongside them.

The Fit

Where a simulator would actually help

Pinellas is the clearest throughput case of the six. The district has vehicles, sites and a funded program, and still delivers driver education as camps roughly once a quarter, explicitly subject to instructor availability.

That phrase is the whole story. The constraint is not money or vehicles. It is certified instructors and calendar time, and neither of those is solved by leasing more cars.

Where it would fit

  • Between camps. Students waiting for a quarterly slot have nothing in the meantime. A simulator program can run in that gap.
  • Increasing reach per instructor. One member of staff can oversee several simulators at once, which is not true of vehicles.
  • Across the sites already identified. The district has already mapped which high schools host driver education, so the rotation is defined.
  • Preparation before a camp. Students arriving at a camp having already covered basics get more from limited behind-the-wheel time.

If you fund driver education through the Dori Slosberg Act. s.318.1215 lets a county add $5 to civil traffic penalties to support driver education, and requires that programs receiving those funds devote at least 30 percent of student instruction time to behind-the-wheel training. Simulator time is not behind-the-wheel time and cannot count toward that 30 percent. We would rather say so now than have it surface during an audit.

That is the most obvious fit. Students waiting for a quarterly slot currently have nothing in the interim.
