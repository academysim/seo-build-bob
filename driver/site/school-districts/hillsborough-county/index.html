The District

What we know about Hillsborough County

Hillsborough County Public Schools is headquartered at 901 E. Kennedy Blvd in Tampa and reported approximately 171,553 non-charter students for 2025 to 2026. The county covers 1,266 square miles, which the district itself notes, and that spread matters for any program delivered across multiple campuses.

Hillsborough runs one of the more established driver education programs in the region. The district describes school-based and summer instruction combining classroom and behind-the-wheel training through DELAP-certified educators, and states that each high school has a DMV-approved driving range. Its program page lists State Farm, the Kiefer Foundation and the Teen Safe Driving Coalition as partners.

Source: district driver education program and district profile.

Please note: Academy Sim Racing does not currently have simulators installed in Florida school districts. The programs described here are services Academy offers and can deploy. Any reference to a district on this site describes an area we can serve, not an existing installation or partnership.

What Academy is, and is not. Academy Sim Racing supplies racing and driving simulators and runs staffed programs with them. We are not a licensed driving school and we do not employ state-certified driver education instructors. Nothing we provide counts toward Florida licensing requirements, the supervised driving hours a learner must complete, or any state-required driver education course. Districts should deliver those through their own certified staff or an appropriately licensed provider. What we offer is supplementary practice and hazard exposure alongside them.

The Fit

Where a simulator would actually help

Hillsborough is the district where we would be least likely to talk about filling a gap, because the program is already there. A district with a driving range at every high school and certified instructors does not need someone explaining why driver education matters.

What a simulator could add here is different. It is the hazard work that a driving range cannot safely stage: a car pulling out, a skid, a genuine emergency stop. A range teaches control in a controlled space. It cannot teach a student what an unexpected hazard feels like at speed.

Where it would fit

  • Ahead of range time. Students arriving having already made the obvious mistakes somewhere with no consequences.
  • For the students in the queue. Behind-the-wheel and range time is rationed. A simulator gives the rest of a cohort something better than waiting.
  • Across 1,266 square miles. A mobile program can rotate between campuses in a way a fixed installation cannot.
  • Alongside existing partners. Safety-focused programming complements rather than competes with the initiatives already running.

This is also our home county. Academy is based at 1421 Massaro Blvd in Tampa, which makes Hillsborough the easiest district in Florida for us to serve and the cheapest to trial.

If you fund driver education through the Dori Slosberg Act. s.318.1215 lets a county add $5 to civil traffic penalties to support driver education, and requires that programs receiving those funds devote at least 30 percent of student instruction time to behind-the-wheel training. Simulator time is not behind-the-wheel time and cannot count toward that 30 percent. We would rather say so now than have it surface during an audit.

Questions

What districts ask

Does Hillsborough already have driver education?
Yes, and a well established one. The district describes school-based and summer instruction with DELAP-certified educators and states that each high school has a DMV-approved driving range. Our programs would supplement that, not replace it.
Do you have simulators in Hillsborough schools?
No. Academy does not currently have simulators installed in any school district. This describes what we can provide.
What would a simulator add to a program that already has a range?
Hazard exposure. A range teaches vehicle control in a controlled space. It cannot safely stage a car pulling out, a skid or a genuine emergency stop, and those are what a simulator does well.
Can you cover multiple campuses?
Yes. A mobile program rotates between schools, which suits a district spread across 1,266 square miles better than a single fixed installation would.
You are based in Hillsborough. Does that change anything?
Practically, yes. We are at 1421 Massaro Blvd in Tampa, so this is the district with the shortest logistics and the lowest travel cost for a trial.
How would we start?
Usually a single day on one campus. It tells a district more than a proposal does, and it is the cheapest way to find out whether the format suits your students.
