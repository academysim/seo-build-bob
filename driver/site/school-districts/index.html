What districts are actually solving

Every Florida district has the same structural constraint. Behind-the-wheel instruction needs a car, a certified instructor and a road, and those three things cap how many students can be trained no matter how much a district wants to train more.

A simulator does not remove that constraint, and anyone claiming otherwise is overselling. What it does is add capacity for the parts of driver education that do not require a public road, and let students experience hazards that cannot be staged safely at all.

What we hear districts are working against

  • Throughput. More students want the course than the vehicle fleet supports.
  • Instructor availability. Certified instructors are the binding constraint in most districts, not equipment.
  • Coverage. Programs that exist at some schools and not others.
  • Hazard exposure. The situations that cause crashes are the ones you cannot practice on a road with a permit holder.

Please note: Academy Sim Racing does not currently have simulators installed in Florida school districts. The programs described here are services Academy offers and can deploy. Any reference to a district on this site describes an area we can serve, not an existing installation or partnership.

What Academy is, and is not. Academy Sim Racing supplies racing and driving simulators and runs staffed programs with them. We are not a licensed driving school and we do not employ state-certified driver education instructors. Nothing we provide counts toward Florida licensing requirements, the supervised driving hours a learner must complete, or any state-required driver education course. Districts should deliver those through their own certified staff or an appropriately licensed provider. What we offer is supplementary practice and hazard exposure alongside them.

If you are with a district not listed, get in touch. The answer is almost certainly yes.

If you fund driver education through the Dori Slosberg Act. s.318.1215 lets a county add $5 to civil traffic penalties to support driver education, and requires that programs receiving those funds devote at least 30 percent of student instruction time to behind-the-wheel training. Simulator time is not behind-the-wheel time and cannot count toward that 30 percent. We would rather say so now than have it surface during an audit.

Context

Florida teen licensing, briefly

Worth having straight when planning a program. Under Florida law a learner license is available from age 15, and a Class E license from 16 requires the learner license to have been held for at least 12 months and at least 50 hours of supervised driving, of which 10 must be at night. Sixteen year olds may not drive between 11pm and 6am, and seventeen year olds not between 1am and 5am, with exceptions for work or when accompanied by a licensed driver aged 21 or over.

Source: Florida Driver License Handbook and s.322.05. Confirm current requirements with FLHSMV rather than relying on this summary.

Questions

What districts ask

Do you have simulators in Florida school districts already?
No. Academy does not currently have simulators installed in any school district. Everything on this site describes programs we offer and can deploy, not existing installations.
Can simulator time replace behind-the-wheel instruction?
No. It supplements classroom and behind-the-wheel instruction. If your program is funded under the Dori Slosberg Act, note that at least 30 percent of student time must be behind the wheel, and simulator time does not count toward it.
Which districts can you serve?
Any Florida district. We are based in Tampa and travel statewide. The six with pages here are simply where we have written detail first.
How does a district buy this?
Tell us how your district procures and we will work to it. Quotes, W-9s, insurance certificates and vendor registration usually take longer than scheduling does, so raise it early.
Can we trial it before committing?
Yes, and we would suggest it. A single day on one campus tells a district more than a proposal does.
What does it cost?
It depends on student numbers, duration, number of simulators and travel. We quote per program rather than publishing a price.

Which district are you with?

Tell us the district, the schools involved and rough student numbers. We will come back with options and costs.
