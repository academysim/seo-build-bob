The skills that actually keep new drivers safe

Passing a test and being a safe driver are different achievements. These pages cover the skills that sit in the gap, what each one involves, and how a simulator can and cannot help.

The gap between licensed and safe

Florida gives a learner license from age 15 and a Class E license from 16, provided the learner license has been held at least 12 months and the driver has completed at least 50 hours of supervised driving including 10 at night. Sixteen year olds may not drive between 11pm and 6am, and seventeen year olds not between 1am and 5am, with exceptions for work or when accompanied by a licensed driver aged 21 or over.

Fifty hours is a real requirement and it is also not very much. What those hours contain depends entirely on what happened to come up, which means most new drivers get licensed having never experienced the situations that actually cause crashes.

These pages cover those situations. Confirm current licensing requirements with FLHSMV rather than relying on this summary.

Topics

The skills

HAZARD RECOGNITION

Seeing the developing problem rather than the current one.

DEFENSIVE DRIVING

Following distance, positioning and assuming less about other drivers.

EMERGENCY BRAKING

Most drivers do not brake hard enough when it counts.

RAIN AND WET ROADS

Florida-specific, and the conditions most new drivers meet unprepared.

NIGHT DRIVING

Ten of the required 50 hours, and the hardest to arrange.

MANUAL TRANSMISSION

Increasingly rare, still occasionally essential.

Please note: Academy Sim Racing does not currently have simulators installed in Florida school districts. The programs described here are services Academy offers and can deploy. Any reference to a district on this site describes an area we can serve, not an existing installation or partnership.

What Academy is, and is not. Academy Sim Racing supplies racing and driving simulators and runs staffed programs with them. We are not a licensed driving school and we do not employ state-certified driver education instructors. Nothing we provide counts toward Florida licensing requirements, the supervised driving hours a learner must complete, or any state-required driver education course. Districts and schools should deliver those through their own certified staff or an appropriately licensed provider. What we offer is supplementary practice and hazard exposure alongside them.

Questions

What districts ask

Are these programs for individuals or schools?
Both. Schools and districts bring these in as programs. Individual teen training is booked through Academy directly.
Does simulator practice count toward the 50 supervised hours?
No. The 50 hours are supervised driving on real roads. Simulator time is preparation for them, not a substitute.
Which skill matters most?
Hazard recognition, in our view. Most of the others are responses to a hazard, and seeing it earlier makes every one of them easier.
Can a nervous teenager start on a simulator?
Yes, and it is one of the better uses. Getting the initial unfamiliarity out of the way somewhere with no consequences makes the first real drive calmer.
Do you work with parents directly?
Yes. Teen training is available through Academy. Contact us and we will explain how it works.
Do you have simulators in schools already?
No. Academy does not currently have simulators installed in any school district. These are programs we offer and can deploy.

Want help with any of these?

Parents, students and schools all ask about these. Tell us the situation and we will tell you what we can do.
