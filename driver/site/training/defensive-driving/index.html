Defensive Driving

Defensive Driving

Defensive driving is a phrase that has been used so loosely it has almost stopped meaning anything. In practice it comes down to giving yourself time and assuming less about everyone else.

Why It Matters

The problem

Most crashes involving new drivers are not caused by a lack of vehicle control. They happen because the driver had no time and no options when something went wrong, and they had no time because of decisions made several seconds earlier.

Defensive driving is the set of habits that preserve time and options. It is unglamorous and it is the most valuable thing a new driver can learn.

The Skill

What good looks like

Following distance

Almost every new driver follows more closely than they think. Following distance is the single biggest lever on whether a driver has time to respond, and it costs nothing.

Road position

Where the car sits in a lane changes what the driver can see and what others can see of them. Most new drivers have never thought about it.

Assuming less

The dangerous assumption is that other road users will do the correct thing. Defensive driving is largely the habit of leaving room for them not to.

Having an out

Experienced drivers are continuously aware of where they would go if they had to. New drivers usually are not, and it is a teachable habit.

Practicing It

Where a simulator helps, and where it does not

Where it helps. A simulator can show a student what their actual following distance is and what it costs when the car ahead brakes hard. That is a difficult lesson to arrange safely any other way.

Where it does not. Defensive driving is a habit rather than a skill, and habits form through repetition over months of real driving. A session can start the habit. It cannot install it.

Questions

What districts ask

What does defensive driving actually mean?
In practice: following distance, road position, assuming less about other drivers, and always knowing where you would go if you had to. It is about preserving time and options.
Is it worth teaching before a student can drive?
The concepts, yes. The habits need real driving to form, but understanding them first makes those first hours more productive.
Does it satisfy a court or district requirement?
Not that we can claim. If it is meant to meet a specific requirement, confirm that with whoever set it before planning around our program.
How is this different from hazard recognition?
Hazard recognition is seeing the problem. Defensive driving is having arranged things so you have time and room to deal with it.
Can you run this for students who already drive?
Yes, and that is often the better audience, because they have habits to correct rather than none yet formed.
Do you have simulators in schools already?
No. Academy does not currently have simulators installed in any school district.
