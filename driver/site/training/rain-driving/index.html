Rain And Wet Roads

Rain and Wet Roads

In Florida this is not an edge case. Afternoon storms are a routine part of the year, and a new driver will meet heavy rain long before they have the experience to handle it.

Why It Matters

The problem

Florida gives new drivers two things at once: a lot of sudden heavy rain, and a licensing timeline that does not guarantee they will have driven in any of it before passing.

A student can complete 50 supervised hours in dry weather and be licensed without ever having driven on a genuinely wet road. The first time is then unsupervised.

The Skill

What good looks like

The first few minutes are the worst

Rain lifts oil and rubber off the surface before washing it away, so grip is lowest shortly after rain starts, not during sustained downpour. Most drivers assume the opposite.

Everything takes longer

Braking distances extend and the car responds more slowly. The correct adjustment is more following distance and earlier inputs rather than merely driving slower.

Standing water and aquaplaning

When tires cannot clear water fast enough the car loses contact with the road. The correct response is to ease off and hold the wheel steady rather than brake or steer sharply, which is counterintuitive and worth knowing in advance.

Being seen

Florida law requires headlights when windshield wipers are in continuous use. It also genuinely helps other drivers see you.

Practicing It

Where a simulator helps, and where it does not

Where it helps. A simulator can produce a wet road on demand, which no lesson plan can. A student can experience reduced grip and practice the counterintuitive aquaplaning response repeatedly and safely.

Where it does not. Real rain brings poor visibility, spray from other vehicles and the physical stress of it. Nothing reproduces that fully, and a driver should still get real supervised wet-weather practice.

Questions

What districts ask

When is a wet road most dangerous?
Usually shortly after rain begins, when oil and rubber lift off the surface before being washed away. Many drivers assume sustained heavy rain is worse.
What should a driver do if the car aquaplanes?
Ease off the throttle and hold the wheel steady. Braking or steering sharply makes it worse, which is why it is worth knowing before it happens.
Do headlights have to be on in rain in Florida?
Florida requires headlights when windshield wipers are in continuous use. Confirm current requirements with FLHSMV.
Can a student get real wet-weather practice?
Only if it rains during their supervised hours, which is not something anyone can schedule. That is the gap a simulator fills.
Is this particularly relevant in Florida?
More than in most states. Sudden heavy afternoon rain is routine for much of the year.
Do you have simulators in schools already?
No. Academy does not currently have simulators installed in any school district.
