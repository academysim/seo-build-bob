Manual Transmission

Manual Transmission

Most new drivers will never need this. The ones who do usually need it suddenly, and a parking lot with a borrowed car is a stressful place to find out how a clutch works.

Why It Matters

The problem

Manual transmissions have largely disappeared from American cars, which means a driver can hold a license for years and have no idea how one works. Then they need to move a friend’s car, drive a work vehicle, rent something abroad, or buy a car they like that happens to be manual.

It is a small skill with a steep and embarrassing start. Almost everyone stalls repeatedly at first, and doing that in front of someone whose car it is makes it worse.

The Skill

What good looks like

What the clutch actually does

It disconnects the engine from the wheels so gears can be changed and so the car can start moving from stationary without the engine stopping. Understanding that makes the biting point make sense rather than being a mystery.

Why stalling happens

The clutch is released faster than the engine can pick up the load. The fix is releasing more slowly through the biting point, and adding a little throttle.

Finding the biting point

Every car is slightly different. Finding it is the first thing to learn and the thing that makes everything else possible.

Hills

Starting on an incline is where most learners struggle, because the car rolls back while the clutch is still engaging.

Practicing It

Where a simulator helps, and where it does not

Where it helps. Stalling costs nothing in a simulator, and a learner can do it a hundred times without wear, embarrassment or a queue of traffic behind them. It also means the first attempt in a real car is not the first attempt overall.

Where it does not. The physical feel of a real clutch pedal, and the sound and vibration a car makes as it approaches stalling, are things a driver ultimately has to learn in the car. A simulator shortens that, it does not remove it.

Questions

What districts ask

Is it worth learning manual if my car is automatic?
For most people it sits somewhere between useful and occasionally essential. The people who need it usually need it at short notice.
Why does a car stall?
The clutch is released faster than the engine can take up the load. Releasing more slowly through the biting point, with a little throttle, fixes it.
Can you really learn stick shift on a simulator?
You can learn the sequence, the biting point concept and the coordination without wearing out a real clutch. The final feel has to be learned in a car, and we would not pretend otherwise.
How long does it take?
Most people can move off and change gear within a session. Doing it smoothly on a hill in traffic takes longer.
Do you offer this to individuals?
Yes. Contact us and we will explain how it works.
Do you have simulators in schools already?
No. Academy does not currently have simulators installed in any school district.
