What GT3 Is

GT3 is a technical rulebook rather than a manufacturer or a single championship. Car makers build a racing version of a road-going sports car to that specification, and a number of different series around the world run grids made up of those cars.

Because the cars start from very different road cars, front-engined V8s, mid-engined turbos, flat-sixes, organizers apply what is called Balance of Performance. Parameters such as minimum weight, engine power and aerodynamic configuration are adjusted between models with the aim of bringing their performance closer together. How successfully that works is a permanent topic of argument among people who follow the class, which tells you something about how hard the problem is.

Mechanically the cars sit between a road sports car and a prototype. They generate aerodynamic downforce, run slick tires, and use sequential gearboxes. They also retain anti-lock braking and traction control, which is unusual at this level of motorsport and is a large part of why the class is so widely raced by amateur and professional drivers alike.

This describes the class in general terms. Academy Sim Racing is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or licensed by any racing series, sanctioning body or car manufacturer. Which specific cars and circuits are available depends on the simulator software in use.

How A GT3 Car Differs From A Road Car

Braking

Racing brake discs and pads operating at much higher temperatures than road equipment, with anti-lock braking retained. Braking distances are far shorter than a road car, and the pedal is firmer and less progressive.

Aerodynamics

Downforce rises with speed, so the aerodynamic grip available in a fast corner is greater than in a slow one. This is the single biggest difference from a road car and the hardest thing to develop an instinct for.

Transmission

Sequential gearboxes operated by paddles, so gear changes are a single pull rather than a movement through a gate. If you would rather shift a manual yourself, four of our rigs are fitted for that instead.

Chassis

Stiff springs, minimal body roll and a fixed racing seat. The car changes direction with far less lean than a road car, and curbs register as a sharp hit rather than a rumble.

Which Rig Suits It

We have eight racing simulators and they are not identical. Our library runs iRacing and Assetto Corsa, covering more than 170 cars across more than 140 tracks.

Motion platform

Our flagship pairs a motion platform with triple screens, direct drive steering, active pedals and a real cockpit. Motion communicates load transfer, the car settling under braking, the rear moving on exit, through the seat rather than only through the wheel.

Triple screen

Triple screens widen your field of view, so apexes and mirrors sit where your eyes expect them rather than off the edge of a single display. Paired with direct drive steering and load-cell or active pedals.

Single-screen rigs are also available and run exactly the same software. If a particular configuration matters to you, mention it when you book.

  1. No experience is required

    Basic Coaching ($40) puts an instructor beside you for the whole session, working on braking points, the racing line and the habits that cost time. Advanced Coaching ($60) adds telemetry review for drivers who already have pace. Both are optional extras at checkout.
