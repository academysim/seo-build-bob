The Short Answer

If it is your first time, drive GT3. It has traction control and anti-lock brakes, it shifts with paddles so there is no gearbox to think about, and it has enough downforce to feel stable without being twitchy. That combination is what makes it the sensible first session.

If you already race, or you drive a manual road car and want to use a real gearbox, the rest of this page is the useful part.

The Classes

GT3 · Start Here

Production-based cars built to a balanced ruleset

GT3 cars are derived from road-going models and built to rules designed to keep very different cars competitive with each other. They carry traction control and anti-lock brakes, shift with paddles, and generate enough downforce to feel planted through quick corners without demanding perfect inputs. That combination is why almost every racing academy starts people here.

GT3 simulator sessions in Tampa

Prototype · Endurance

Purpose-built cars that share the circuit with slower traffic

A prototype is built from scratch to a ruleset rather than developed from a road car, and generates more downforce for its weight than a GT car does. In endurance racing the distinctive skill is multi-class awareness: knowing what is behind you, what class it is, and how to pass or be passed without ruining anyone’s race.

Endurance simulator sessions in Tampa

Formula · Least Forgiving

Open-wheel single seaters

Very little weight, a great deal of aerodynamic grip, and in most classes no traction control to catch a mistake with the throttle. Formula cars reward precision and punish everything else. They are the steepest learning curve we offer, and the class most likely to end a first session in a gravel trap.

Formula simulator sessions in Tampa

Stock Car · Oval

Heavy cars with very little aerodynamic help

Built for banked oval racing, stock cars are heavy, powerful and short on downforce. The driving is about managing a car that keeps moving around underneath you, and about running inches from other cars for a long time. It is a completely different discipline from road course racing rather than a variation on it.

Stock car simulator sessions in Tampa

Drift · Sideways

Judged on angle and commitment rather than lap time

Drift cars are set up to be held sideways deliberately. There is no lap time to chase, which makes it the class where getting it wrong costs you nothing, and the fastest way to learn what a car actually does once the rear tires let go. Drift cars use manual or sequential gearboxes depending on the car.

Drift simulator sessions in Tampa

Rally · Against The Clock

One car at a time on a road you do not know

Rally is not wheel-to-wheel racing. You run alone against a clock on a loose surface, working from a co-driver’s calls rather than from memory of the corner ahead. The car spends far more of the time slightly out of shape than it ever does on a circuit, and a handbrake is useful for the tighter corners, though technique varies with the car and the surface.

Rally simulator sessions in Tampa

Classic · No Assistance

Older machinery with nothing to help you

Earlier historic cars tend to combine narrow tires, little aerodynamic downforce and an H-pattern gearbox that has to be used properly. Later classics vary a great deal. What they share is that the driver is given very little help, which is exactly why people who have driven everything else keep coming back to them.

Picking By What You Want

  1. You have never done this before

    GT3 on a circuit you have seen on television. Add Basic Coaching and you will be lapping properly inside the first twenty minutes instead of working it out alone.

  2. You want to use a real gearbox

    Drift or classic. Four of our eight rigs have an H-pattern shifter and a clutch pedal, including the flagship Motion GT, so mention it when you book and we will put you on the right one.

  3. You want the biggest challenge

    Formula. No traction control, very little weight, and aerodynamic load that rises sharply with speed, so the car has most grip exactly where it is least comfortable to trust it.

  4. You are booking for a group

    GT3 across the board. Everyone can drive it, the lap times end up close enough to be competitive, and nobody spends their session spinning while their friends watch.

  5. You have a real track day coming up

    Pick the circuit before the car. Our track guides cover what each one asks of a driver.
