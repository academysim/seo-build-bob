The Benefits

THREE KINDS OF GOOD, ALL AT ONCE

Research consistently shows driving-simulator activity benefits older adults across cognitive, physical, and social dimensions. Unlike a crossword or a walk, it exercises all three at the same time.

01
COGNITIVE BENEFITS
Driving is one of the most cognitively demanding tasks a person does. It engages attention, memory, decision-making, and spatial awareness, simultaneously.
  • Improves divided visual attention
  • Sharpens executive function
  • Exercises working memory
  • Strengthens reaction time
  • Supports MCI intervention
02
PHYSICAL BENEFITS
Simulator driving involves hand-eye coordination, pedal use, and controlled upper-body movement, all repeatable and with no on-road exposure.
  • Maintains fine motor control
  • Practices hand-eye coordination
  • Improves pedal & braking response
  • Encourages postural engagement
  • Zero fall risk, zero road risk
03
SOCIAL & EMOTIONAL
Group sessions create friendly competition, shared stories, and genuine fun. For seniors who've given up driving, the experience can be deeply emotional.
  • Combats social isolation
  • Builds confidence & self-efficacy
  • Provides joyful, purposeful activity
  • Reduces driving-loss depression
  • Creates shared family experiences
Post-Stroke & Recovery

RELEARNING THE ROAD, ONE SAFE LAP AT A TIME

For stroke survivors and those recovering from neurological events, returning to driving is one of the biggest markers of regained independence. Simulators make that path safer, measurable, and more likely to succeed.

The Research

PEER-REVIEWED. PUBLISHED. CITED.

We're not making these claims up, they're rooted in decades of clinical research. A few of the studies behind what we do:

// Cognitive Training · 2014
Driving simulator training increases real driving performance in healthy older drivers
Casutt, Theill, Martin, Keller & Jäncke, a controlled study showing that interactive, multi-task simulator training improves higher-order cognitive functions and everyday abilities in older adults. Published in Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience.
Read the study →
// Post-Stroke · 2005
Effect of simulator training on driving after stroke: a randomized controlled trial
Akinwuntan et al. studied 83 stroke patients randomized to simulator training or cognitive therapy. 73% of the simulator group passed their official pre-driving assessment compared with 42% in the control group. Published in Neurology.
Read the study →
// MCI & Recovery · 2011
Support for stroke patients in resumption of driving: patient survey and simulator trial
Kobayashi et al. demonstrated that repeat simulator training significantly improved braking ability and driving readiness in stroke patients, enhancing rehabilitation and social reintegration outcomes.
Read the study →
// Visual Attention · 2018
Can driving-simulator training enhance visual attention, cognition, and physical functioning in older adults?
Hüttermann, Bock, Memmert, found measurable improvements in divided visual attention in healthy older participants after four weeks of simulator training. Published in the Journal of Aging Research.
Read the study →
A note on scope: Academy Sim Racing provides simulator experiences and structured programs, we are not a medical provider. Clinical outcomes depend on individual health, program design, and the involvement of qualified healthcare professionals. We're happy to partner with your facility's clinical team.
